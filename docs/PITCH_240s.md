# 240-second pitch (NASA Space Apps format)

Beat sheet: Who 45 s, Why 60 s, What 60 s, So what next 75 s. Fill the [brackets]. Practise it under 4:00.

**WHO (0:00-0:45)** Hi, we are [team] from [city]. [One line of team story.] We built **Sol 72**: a game where kids run a Moon or Mars base on real NASA data. A sol is one day on Mars, and you have 72 hours to keep four astronauts alive. Every fix costs something somewhere else. *(Start with the story, not the title.)*

**WHY (0:45-1:45)** Space posters teach facts, not decisions. Real astronauts trade: power for oxygen, shielding for tonight's electricity. Curiosity measured about 0.64 millisieverts of radiation every day on Mars, and one solar storm can add far more. Kids 7 to 12 almost never see that logic, only the result. One killer fact: a message to Mars takes 3 to 22 minutes, so the crew must decide alone.

**WHAT (1:45-2:45)** *(show the demo)* Pick a world and a Sun: today's real flares from NASA DONKI, or the May 2024 storm. A flare is spotted and the banner says particles arrive in four hours. Do you shelter, losing morale and research time, or spend battery on shielding, or gamble? In Commander some flares come from the far side of the Sun with no warning at all. The end screen shows what your choice saved, in real units: "15 mSv instead of 100, 85 % saved". Real InSight weather sets the temperature, JPL orbits set today's radio delay, and every number carries a LIVE or ARCHIVE badge. Data: DONKI, InSight, JPL, POWER, Curiosity RAD, Artemis, OSDR, NASA Image Library.

**SO WHAT NEXT (2:45-4:00)** A child leaves knowing a real constraint (dose, delay, power), not just having played. It works in any browser, no account, in classrooms and at home, and it is replayable: seeds, six different Suns, two worlds, two difficulties. We tested the balance with simulated players: doing nothing earns about one star and starves on Commander, a thoughtful crew earns nearly three. What we need: classroom pilots, a teacher guide, and translations (Bangla first). Close on the base at night with the storm warning on screen, then stop talking.
